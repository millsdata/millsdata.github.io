Question
Where does this retailer's revenue actually come from — by month, market and product — once cancellations and returns are taken out?
Data
UCI Machine Learning Repository, Online Retail dataset: ~542K invoice lines from 1 Dec 2010 to 9 Dec 2011, company anonymized in the original data. Analysis window: the 12 complete months, Dec 2010 – Nov 2011.
Method
A Python script (on GitHub) classifies every line as a Sale, a Return (credit-note invoices) or an Adjustment (zero-price stock movements) rather than deleting messy rows, so the dashboard can explain why sales don't equal revenue. In Tableau, non-product line items — postage, manual entries, discounts, bank charges, Amazon fees — are excluded by stock code, and the partial final month is dropped. Every KPI tile states its basis (gross vs. net); the page has a date-range control and a switchable measure.
Findings

Revenue is a holiday business: monthly net revenue sits between $0.48M and $0.76M for nine months, then September–November deliver 37.5% of the year. November's $1.43M is 2.96× the April low.

The UK is 84.5% of net revenue. The other $1.45M is spread across 37 countries; only the Netherlands, EIRE, Germany and France clear $175K.

Returns are low but lumpy: 3.1% of gross, and a single cancelled 74,215-unit order in January is a quarter of the year's total.
